Why is my Google storage full?

Guide · last updated 20 November 2026

Most Google accounts share one storage pool across Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos. The total can fill up from any of them, and some things that take up space are easy to overlook. This guide explains what counts, what does not, and what happens when the pool is full.

One pool, three services

For a personal Google account, storage is shared across:

  • Gmail: every message you have, including its attachments, in your Inbox, sent folder, drafts, and other folders.
  • Google Drive: files you own or have stored in Drive, including the files you upload and the versions Drive keeps of them.
  • Google Photos: photos and videos you back up. Google's rules about which photo quality counts toward storage have changed over the years, so check Google's current help page if this matters to you.

Because the three share a total, a full Drive can make Gmail look full too, and the reverse is also true.

Things that count that people often miss

Trash

Items in Gmail's Trash and Spam, and files in Drive's Trash, still count toward your storage. They stay until they are removed. Google has described a 30-day period after which these items are deleted automatically, but emptying Trash frees space immediately.

Old versions of files

For many non-Google file types, Drive keeps earlier versions of a file, and those versions use space. Some versions can be kept indefinitely if they are marked to keep forever. Google's documentation describes limits on how many versions are kept and for how long, and these limits can change. You can review a file's versions from its menu in Drive.

Large attachments in old mail

A message sent to a group with a large file, followed by a long reply chain, can store the same attachment many times. Each copy in a received message can count toward your storage.

Things that usually do not count

Files shared with you

Files that other people share with you generally do not count toward your storage, because the owner's account holds the file. If you make your own copy of a shared file, the copy does count.

Native Google documents

Documents, spreadsheets, and presentations created in Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides have generally not counted toward storage, while uploaded files in other formats do. This policy is set by Google and may change, so check the current help pages if it matters for a decision.

Per-user limits in Workspace

If your account is part of a Google Workspace organisation (for example a school, university, or company), your administrator may set storage differently. Some organisations pool storage across all users, and others set a limit for each user. Your administrator can show you the limit that applies to your account. Storage used in a shared drive belongs to the organisation rather than to you personally.

How to see what is using the space

  1. Open the storage page in your Google account (you can reach it from Drive settings, at drive.google.com/settings/storage).
  2. Note which service uses the most space.
  3. Look at Gmail for large messages, using the search operators in the guide to large emails.
  4. Look at Drive for large files and duplicates, following the guide to duplicate files.
  5. Empty Trash and Spam once you are sure nothing in them is needed.

What happens when storage is full

Google's help pages say that when an account is over its limit, you may not be able to send or receive email, upload new files to Drive, or back up new photos, until you free up space or buy more storage. The exact behaviour, including any grace period, depends on the service and your account type, so read Google's current help for the details. Google also warns in the storage page when you are close to the limit, which gives you time to act.

Avoid rushed deletions. When storage fills suddenly, it is tempting to delete everything large at once. Work through the largest items first, check each one, and keep a note of anything important you remove.

Buying more storage

Google sells additional storage plans. Buying more space is a valid choice, but it helps to know what is using the space first, because a clean-up may be cheaper and may fix the cause.

Tracking usage with Gmail Storage Lens

Gmail Storage Lens is an open-source tool that is designed to show the split between Gmail and Drive, and to help you plan a clean-up with a preview before anything changes. It runs in your browser, reads metadata only, and sends data only to Google. The project is still under development, so check its current status before relying on specific features.
